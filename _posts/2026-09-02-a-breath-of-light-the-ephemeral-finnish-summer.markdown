Those who have lived in the North their entire lives say summer here is not a season on the calendar, but a state of mind. And they are entirely right. A Finnish summer is a flash of brilliant light as breathtaking as it is brief—a golden truce that lasts barely a couple of weeks of genuine warmth before drawing back into the twilight.

<!--more-->

### Two Weeks of Golden Truce

In southern latitudes, summer is a leisurely, months-long sprawl where sweltering heat turns into a predictable daily backdrop. In Finland, by contrast, summer is an urgent celebration. The instant the thermometer crosses twenty degrees Celsius, life shifts into hyperdrive. It is a shared, beautiful exuberance: midnight swimmers plunge into crystal lakes, restaurant terraces across Helsinki overflow with laughter, and ancient pine forests take on a green so electric it borders on surreal.

Yet this year, the truce proved faithful once more to its ephemeral nature. We were granted merely two glorious weeks of unclouded sun—of feeling the warm air against bare arms and tasting the magic of an endless day. Then, as swiftly as an eclipse when you blink, the heat evaporated.

### September and the Retreat of Daylight

You can already taste it in the morning air. The dawn breeze carries an icy sharpness that prompts you to shut the balcony window and reach for the wool sweater packed away at the back of the wardrobe in an excess of summertime optimism. Temperatures have begun their steady, predictable decline—a familiar choreography we know all too well.

Yet the most dramatic shift is etched not in the thermometer, but across the sky. September has arrived, and with it, the swift retreat of light. It is staggering how, in mere days, the nightless days of the midnight sun collapse. Sunset creeps earlier and earlier. Afternoons noticeably shorten day by day, and that deep velvet darkness we thought banished reasserts its dominion, signaling that the season of inward retreat has returned.

### The Looming White Shadow

In Finland, autumn is never a lazy, protracted interlude; it is the immediate foyer of the northern giant. Winter doesn't stroll in politely—it hangs over us like a colossal shadow poised to descend. The atmosphere is charged with a poignant cocktail of melancholy and poetic introspection. Birch leaves are turning vibrant amber and crimson—nature's last defiant blaze before the grand white silence descends.

For those of us drawn to stillness and cold, this metamorphosis carries its own profound magic. It is time once more to light candles, brew steaming coffee, stoke the sauna with greater frequency, and attune the spirit to the inward journey winter demands.

Summer was only a breath—a fleeting gift that slips through our fingers. Yet perhaps that fleeting nature is precisely what lends it infinite worth. If it were everlasting, we could never love it with such fierce devotion.

Winter is already peeking around the corner of September, ready to drape everything in its crystalline mantle. And here, once again, we stand ready to welcome it.

> *"What is summer? It is a fleeting arc of warmth that rises and falls in an instant, leaving you with the memory of a sun that never quite managed to set."*
