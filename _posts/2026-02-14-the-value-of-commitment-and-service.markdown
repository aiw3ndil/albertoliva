Modern life constantly nudges us toward self-indulgence, the perpetual hunt for personal gratification, and coddling our own ego. But what happens when our actions intersect with the needs of others? It is easy to fall into the trap of "pretending we work for others, when in reality we haven't surrendered our ego to do so." This piercing phrase captures an internal battle many of us face: the friction between the self and the collective, between personal whim and communal responsibility.

<!--more-->

The true core of the matter lies in the authenticity of our devotion. **When you do something for others, you must commit 100%. You cannot pledge your word and then fail that person.** A half-hearted commitment is not merely ineffective—it corrodes trust and weakens the fabric of human relationship. It is not a shallow transaction, but a covenant—implicit or stated—that our energy and full intent are dedicated to a shared cause or person.

For many, words like "duty" or "mission" sound like burdensome chains. Yet, **your duty should be the most meaningful pillar in your life.** This does not mean forced martyrdom, but living with purpose. If your job, or your role in society, brings you no fulfillment and fails to resonate with who you are, then **if you dislike your work, do not do it—change it. But failing to show up is no answer.** Passivity and resting on mediocrity only sustain an untenable situation, both for yourself and for those you are meant to serve. The way forward is not shirking accountability, but finding responsibilities you can embrace with all your being.

Ultimately, **you must be of service to others, fulfilling your mission alongside your fellow human beings. Life cannot remain so self-absorbed.** Human existence, in its deepest reality, is enriched and illuminated through mutual connection. When we live solely for ourselves, we shut out the profound wealth born from lifting others. The conviction that **life is meant to be given in service to our fellow human beings** is a potent reminder that fulfillment is found in transcending the ego and aligning our will with the greater good. In that selfless offering, we find not only true utility, but lasting, profound peace.
