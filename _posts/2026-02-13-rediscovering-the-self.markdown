We find ourselves in a perpetual pursuit. We search for happiness, purpose, success, inner peace. We throw ourselves into an endless journey toward a destination we imagine external to ourselves, oblivious to the fact that the most fundamental answer already dwells within. The question is not how to find Being, but: why have we lost awareness of it?

<!--more-->

**There is nothing to seek and nothing to find.** This simple yet profound realization is the starting point. You know that you are. You harbor no doubt whatsoever about your own existence at this very instant. It is a certainty so intrinsic and fundamental that we routinely overlook it, drowned out by the noise of our thoughts, cravings, and anxieties. Existing is not an achievement; it is our natural baseline.

The real challenge, then, is not the search, but maintaining awareness. **The only thing you need do is remain anchored in the sensation of being.** It is an act of grounding in the present, an unbroken return to the pure consciousness of "I am" prior to any subsequent label or qualification: "I am a developer," "I am a parent," "I am happy," "I am sad." Before all of that, simply: "I am."

Why, then, have we lost touch with it? The answer lies in what ancient sages and philosophers called **Maya**: the veil of illusion. Maya is not an external, malevolent force, but the very fabric of our unobserved mind. It is the stories we tell ourselves, the relentless distractions of the modern landscape, our fixation with ego identity and worldly possessions. It is the belief that we are our thoughts, rather than the silent watcher behind them.

When we entangle ourselves in Maya, our attention fragments. We live stranded in memories of the past or trapped in anxiety about the future, forsaking the only realm where life actually transpires: the now. In doing so, we lose contact with that "sense of being"—which is ever-present, ever-serene, ever-complete.

Taking Maya seriously means deciphering its game. It means watching how the mind darts restlessly from thought to thought, how transient emotions pull us in, and how societal conditioning imposes scripts that distance us from our core. By observing this mechanism directly, we begin to disentangle ourselves from it.

The way back requires no roadmap, only stillness. It demands not more knowledge, but less noise. It is the simple, gentle act of returning, time and again, to the quiet, miraculous certainty that you already are.
