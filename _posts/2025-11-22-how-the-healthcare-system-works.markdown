One of the most striking things when you arrive in Finland is how **organized, clear, and accessible** its healthcare and social system is. It isn't perfect, but it is designed so that anyone—including immigrants undergoing the integration process—can receive medical care and social support when needed.

<!--more-->

In this post, I explain how it works, what to expect, and how it is used in real life.

## Public Healthcare: Terveyskeskus

The Finnish healthcare system is managed by the **wellbeing services counties (Hyvinvointialueet)**. The gateway is always the **Terveyskeskus**, your local health center.

There, you can access:

* General medical care
* Nursing care
* Mental health services
* Vaccinations
* Prescription renewals
* Referrals to hospital specialists

For any non-urgent issue, you first call or book an appointment with nursing staff. They assess your situation and assign a doctor if necessary.

**Important:**
Even if you hold a residence permit or are currently studying Finnish, **you have the right to public healthcare**. You only need your personal identity code (henkilötunnus) and your Kela card if you already have it.

## Medicines and Electronic Prescriptions

In Finland, all prescriptions are **electronic**. You can view them on:

* Kanta.fi
* Pharmacy mobile apps
* Or by presenting your ID card at any pharmacy

Kela covers a portion of the price of many medications, so when you purchase a prescription, the reimbursement discount is applied automatically at checkout.

## Hospital Care

If a health center doctor determines you need a specialist, they will refer you to the **regional hospital**. Wait times depend on the specific condition, but Finland prioritizes:

* Severe medical issues
* Complex diagnoses
* Moderate or severe mental health conditions
* Necessary surgeries

In emergencies, call **112**, and you will be directed straight to the hospital.

## Mental Health

Finland takes mental health seriously. You can access:

* Free brief short-term therapy
* Health center psychologists
* Psychiatrists via referral
* Rehabilitation plans if required

Additionally, Kela can subsidize **long-term psychotherapy** if justified by a healthcare professional.

## Social System: Benefits and Financial Support

Kela is the agency that manages virtually all social benefits. These include:

### ✓ Unemployment Benefit

If you are enrolled in an integration course, you can receive financial support while studying.

### ✓ Basic Social Assistance

For individuals with low income.

### ✓ General Housing Allowance

If you rent your home, part of your rent can be covered.

### ✓ Healthcare Reimbursements

Medicines, medical transport, specific medical treatments.

### ✓ Rehabilitation

Medical, psychological, or vocational rehabilitation programs to maintain or restore your work capacity.

All of this is applied for online at **kela.fi**, and while bureaucratic, the process is far more transparent than in many other countries.

## Social Work (Sosiaalityö)

If you encounter issues related to:

* Finances
* Mental health
* Debt
* Integration
* Housing
* Challenging day-to-day situations

You can schedule an appointment with a **social worker**. Their role is to help stabilize your situation and guide you through the system.

## A System Grounded in Prevention and Stability

What stands out most about Finland is that the system is designed to:

* Prevent issues before they worsen
* Help you maintain financial stability
* Guarantee access to medicines and essential treatments
* Support you during health or employment hardships

You don't have to battle against the system: it is structured so you can access it without undue barriers.

## Conclusion

Living in Finland can be a challenge, especially in the beginning. But having such a structured healthcare and social safety net gives you the feeling that **you are not alone**—that a solid support network exists while you build your new life.

Whether you're learning Finnish, looking for a job, or simply settling in, understanding how this system works is a huge advantage.
